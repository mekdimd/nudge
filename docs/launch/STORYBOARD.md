# Launch storyboard

The README also explains desktop app launching through a labelled Dock mockup and local vision through an orange OCR overlay. These still images sit in Photos and do not add shots to the film.

The film follows one focal point at a time. Each demo begins with the task above a large Nudge window. Processing stays on Nudge. Browser actions get the entire stage, with a blue cursor and a slight camera move toward its target. Captions occupy the same position throughout the demos.

1. Three quick misses happen on the bank website. Red click marks show where the person missed. The website dims, then "Hard to click?" appears in the center. A blue cursor arrives, clicks Statements and carries into the reveal as the website fades away. Follow the cursor throughout this transition.
2. The blue pointer stops beside "Meet Nudge." The title settles, then holds as the two-line tagline appears. The pointer keeps the same position, scale and artwork across the cut.
3. "Say 'Hey Nudge' to start." The highlighted phrase appears word by word beside animated voice bars. Nudge's actual bar changes to listening, its orb responds and the request appears as a partial transcript. "Then say what you need" guides the viewer into the spoken bank request. Focus on the bar, with no browser competing for attention.
4. "Download a bank statement." The goal appears in the large Nudge bar. "Nudge finds the next control" accompanies its loading indicator. Focus moves to a continuous bank recording for Statements, View statement and Download PDF. Each cursor arrival has a visible click. A green "Statement downloaded!" badge and the completed step feed close the demo.
5. "Play an Excel tutorial on YouTube." Start with the request in Nudge, then show Gemini drafting the query under "Gemini handles natural language." Focus moves to YouTube's search field while Nudge types. A blue trail follows the pointer into the suggestion and tutorial clicks. The selected tutorial plays under a green "Video playing!" badge.
6. "Enable Peek." Introduce the full Nudge bar, zoom toward its Peek button and click it in the same window. The enabled state holds briefly, then focus moves straight to the browser. Focus moves to the dark-mode VS Code issue tracker, where the actual control outlines draw across the page. "Peek draws debug boxes around UI items" explains the guided control close-ups. Focus returns to the large Nudge feed and its loading indicator while "Jev is TypeSafe AI's action model" identifies the model. A blue highlight guides the eye to the recorded processing row. Its Labels decision appears in the feed. The browser returns with a spotlight on Labels. Pause before the click. A dark diagram shows the goal and four candidate controls under "Jev ranks options and selects the highest probability." The left screenshot stays dim while the probability bars grow. Labels wins in mint at 55%; the other choices dim. A blue line draws from the winning Labels row back to its debug box as the screenshot brightens. Hold the finished diagram for three seconds, then return to the browser. The cursor resumes and clicks Labels. The scores are labelled "Example probabilities." Peek draws the boxes; Jev ranks the labelled actions.
7. "Write an email to your professor." Introduce the mail client, then show Compose opening. Focus moves to Nudge while it chooses the next steps and generates the draft. "Nudge reads approval prompts aloud" accompanies the spoken draft prompt. "Edit the draft before Nudge types it" follows above the review panel. A subject-field close-up shows the black cursor selecting "Absent" and replacing it with "Missing," with typing sounds. The complete edited draft returns before the person chooses Type it once. Focus moves to the mail client under "Nudge types it for you." Its recipient, subject and body fill in sequence, then the complete email holds. Focus returns to Nudge for "Approve sending the email" and its spoken Send prompt. The person chooses Yes, send once. The browser returns under "Then Nudge clicks Send," the blue pointer clicks Send and the local result holds.
8. Nudge's pointer, wordmark and repository address close the film. The tagline has two lines: "Computer control for" and "limited dexterity."

The cursor flights remain quick. The longer cut gives the task, processing, approvals and results time to register. The film uses edited timing and reconstructed target movement. Bank and mail contain fictitious data, and no email is sent. The wake shot replays voice callbacks into the actual UI and loads the repository's Hey Nudge model. It does not test a microphone or speech recognition.
